# Tempered Blade — Crit Ability Design Patterns

**Cycle**: 300 | **Date**: April 2026

---

## Gen 3 Crit Math — The Visibility Problem

Gen 3 crit stages: 0=6.25%, 1=12.5%, 2=25%, 3=33%, 4=50%. A simple +1 stage ability (like Super Luck, which doesn't exist in Gen 3) only bumps from 6.25% to 12.5% — practically invisible to the player over a normal playthrough. Players won't **feel** the ability unless crits happen noticeably often.

The combo matters: if Iron Leaf (signature move) already has high-crit flag (+1 stage), then Tempered Blade (+1 more) pushes to stage 2 (25%) — one in four. That's noticeable. Without the move's own crit boost, the ability alone is too subtle.

## ROM Hack Precedents

- **Emerald Exceeded**: Buffed Hyper Cutter to include crit rate increase — same "physical attacker + crit" archetype. Received positively because it stacked with Focus Energy and high-crit moves.
- **Elite Redux**: Custom abilities succeed when they change what a species CAN DO, not just boost numbers. A crit ability that only works on Steel contact moves is narrow but thematic — it forces a playstyle (use Iron Leaf, not just any STAB).
- **Competitive consensus** (Smogon threads): Crit-stacking sets are "second choice to more dependable sets" in PvP but in single-player ROM hacks, the RNG excitement is actually a feature — crits feel rewarding against AI opponents.

## Key Insight

Crit abilities are the rare case where single-player context HELPS: crits feel exciting against AI. But the floor still matters. If Tempered Blade is +1 crit stage and Iron Leaf is a flat power move without high-crit, the ability fires at 12.5% — indistinguishable from noise over a gym battle. Either Iron Leaf needs high-crit intrinsically, or Tempered Blade should grant +2 stages on qualifying moves.
