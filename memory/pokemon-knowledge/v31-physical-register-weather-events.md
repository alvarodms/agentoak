## C378 — next arc "acting on the ink costs someone something visible" (searched Nov 2026; opinion-grade)
- **No game found where the player's *true* record, over-extended by others, harms NPCs**; no Pokémon hack either (PokéCommunity "moral choices" thread asks for such hacks and gets few answers; StunningSteel = rival-relationship choices → 3 endings). LoH would be ahead of the scene again; no reception data.
- **Guilt needs self-attribution** (Frontiers in Psychology 2021, guilt in games): harm moves the player only if they tie it to their own act. **But** Cain: NPCs react to the *outcome*, not to who caused it, or they read as omniscient; players still read outcome reactions as "the world noticed me". So: the cost is staged on a place/object the player already saw built on their ink (e.g. the Fortree dolls), and no NPC names the player, the notebook or Birch.
- **Cost must touch play or it's dismissed** (Game Developer / moral-reflection pieces): a purely cosmetic sad tile gets shrugged off (cf. C356: silent traces go unnoticed). Prefer a lost service/fight/item the player can feel, kept small.
- **"Blamed for being railroaded" risk** (TV Tropes): inking is automatic on catch, so a cost aimed at the player's choice punishes a non-choice. Frame the cost as the believers' over-reach of a correct fact (WREN's "so they keep it off. Right?"), never as the player being wrong; avoid moralising (multiple sources: preachy consequence reads insincere).

## C375 — v3.5 storm charms: real-world weather dolls, doll objects on grass (searched Nov 2026)
- **Teru teru bōzu is the real "storm charm"** (Wikipedia, TV Tropes, Stripes Japan): a white cloth/paper doll that children hang at a window to ask for fair weather. Hung upside down it asks for rain instead (called "fure fure / ame ame bōzu"; the names differ by source). It is hard to balance and often flips by accident. That fits LoH's "proud and slightly wrong": the folk charm is believed for its facing and does nothing physical. Facing *is* the meaning, so all dolls must share one facing (FACE_LEFT toward Route 119), and only one cue should show it. Don't name teru teru bōzu in game text: Hoenn has its own folklore, and naming it would turn show into tell.
- **No source on doll-object readability on grass.** In vanilla, Pokémon dolls are only Secret Base decorations (Serebii LOTAD DOLL; Bulbapedia Pokémon doll), and no hack thread puts them on a route or in a town. So the screenshot is the only real evidence. Engine note (pokered disasm; Gen III works the same way): the grass-over-sprite priority drop applies only on tall-grass behaviours. Fortree 1,7/2,7/4,7 are MB_NORMAL, so nothing covers the dolls. The risk is contrast (green Lotad on green grass), not occlusion.

## C356 — v3.2 T1 "ring in the ash": silent persistent traces (searched Nov 2026)
- **Silent worn-path traces mostly go unnoticed.** Death Stranding's player-worn trails (a faint trail becomes a path with use; Sony patent "gradual building of a route") were "a secret feature most players missed" in DS1 (Gfinity, 2025). Even a AAA trace with no text was missed by most players. T1 is a bonus for observant players. Don't count it as proof the arc landed. Measure it only with an open recall question ("did anything on Route 113 look different on your second visit?"), never by naming the ring.
- **Indexical storytelling** (Fernández-Vara, GDC 2012 / DiGRA): a trace reads as story only when the player can tie the effect to a cause they remember. The DiGRA paper notes players mostly pick traces up in passing and seldom search like "archaeologists". So the ring must be on screen the moment the player arrives, with nothing to find. Its value is that it was there before the player stepped. The checklist step should check it on the first frame after the warp, before the player walks: the player's own fresh steps leave the same 0x212 and would hide it.
- No public pokeemerald doc covers setmetatile persistence. The pret wiki only says metatile changes are "most common during map load" and that `wait*` commands in level scripts hardlock. That confirms the ON_LOAD choice and rules out any waitstate/delay in Route113_OnLoad.

## C353 — Post-v3.1 planning: leaving the "…?" open, luring players to a quiet beat (searched Nov 2026; opinion-grade)
- **Ambiguity holds up only when it's optional and the player still gets a payoff.** Game Developer's Dear Esther piece: the open story was praised, but players who *invested in interpreting* it were "frustrated or offended" to find there was no answer. Wayline: unresolved ambiguity that "seems arbitrary" reads as being cheated. The Witness (Blow): hidden layers reward only the players who dig, and the core stays complete. Rule for the page-3 "…?": keep it open (C347), but never make the doubt look like it asks for an action, e.g. a new lead, item or location. An open doubt that sends the player searching becomes Dear Esther's "no solution" trap.
- **Vanilla Gen III signposting by TV outbreak is random and gets missed.** Outbreaks reach the TV through record mixing/daily rolls (Bulbapedia Mass outbreak; Smogon Gen 3 swarms thread). A PokéBase player saw none in about two months. So pointing players to Route 106 with a random TV/Match Call hint repeats the vanilla failure. A pointer must be deterministic: a fixed NPC line or a place the player already passes.
- **Scene split, 2026** (PokéCommunity "future of romhacks", 468 FR/EM hacks since 2015): Story/Region (Unbound-like) vs Difficulty/QoL (Radical Red-like). r/PokemonROMhacks Jan 2026 "best 2025 story" thread praised *Odyssey* for narrative + **pacing**. A May 2026 dev post (*Ambrosia* HP/damage display) drew mixed feedback, which shows info-heavy UI is contested. LoH sits in the story camp, where pacing is what gets praised, not volume.
- Sources: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/ambiguity-in-game-design-what-developers-can-learn-from-dear-esther , https://www.wayline.io/blog/ambiguous-endings-in-games , https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-for-a-sense-of-mystery-and-wonder , https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mass_outbreak , https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/86975/when-can-i-expect-an-outbreak-in-pokemon-emerald-gba , https://www.pokecommunity.com/threads/the-future-of-pok%C3%A9mon-romhacks.536951/

## C371 — v3.5 "loud moment": what the player cannot step around (searched Nov 2026; opinion-grade, no numbers)
- **Critical path = the only guaranteed audience.** Avowed/BG3 devs (GamesRadar) say outright that most players never see most optional content; bugnet/Game Developer "Hinterlands": the critical path is the route ALL players walk. No reliable % exists (a "70% never see it" claim is unsourced). Pair with C360: most players never reach postgame. A beat gated on optional/den/postgame state is not "unavoidable", whatever its staging.
- **"Loud" must not mean a long forced cutscene.** PokéCommunity "what quit moment made you drop a hack" names a long unskippable scene; itch.io players took an unskippable opening for a softlock. Unavoidable = it sits on the path (a body in the way, a fight to pass), not a lockall monologue.
- **Vanilla precedent on the exact map**: Emerald's invisible Kecleon blocks the Fortree Gym door until the Devon Scope (Route 120, Steven): a critical-path obstruction the player physically meets (from game knowledge; the web search found no reception data; verify FortreeCity scripts). Fortree also holds the C344 crayon kid, so a v3.5 Fortree beat must not stack on it.

## C364/C366 — Hours 3-6 (Dewford→Mauville) register (searched Nov 2026; thin)
- **No hack found where an early gym fight or dialogue reacts to what the player has caught or learned** (PokéCommunity, Romhacking.net, r/PokemonROMhacks). Mid-game hacks change rosters, levels and gauntlets (Emerald Encore, Emerald But Bad, Imperium), never what the player *knows*. LoH is ahead of the scene, with no reception data.
- **Dewford Gym** is pitch-black in vanilla; the light radius grows per trainer beaten, the room lights when Brawly falls, and Flash is barred (Bulbapedia Darkness / Thonky). That is a vanilla "fight changes the place" beat. No player polls on the darkness turned up; the only gripe found (itch.io) was backtracking to Dewford after Granite Cave (a page-3 map, so D1 applies next door).
- **Routes 105-109: Water-trainer glut is the known Emerald mid-game flaw** (Emerald Horizons review thread: "inherited from vanilla… short of changing their typing, no fix"). A reactive beat there that swaps one sea trainer's team, or makes them react to a Changed type, also breaks monotony. IVAN (C364) already used a fisher + rain, so don't repeat that.
- **Tim Cain, "believable reactivity"**: NPCs should react to *results* the NPC could plausibly see (a carried item, a mon in the party, a changed place), not to actions they never witnessed, or they read as omniscient. Hours 3-6 strangers never met the P1/P2 believers, so they shouldn't key on dex flags or quote the notebook. Key them on the party/lead species (C329 "show me" grammar) or on a place change. Tiering also helps: one NPC remembers in detail, the rest only see the result.
- Boss-design writing (Game Developer "3 elements"): a fight should test what the player already learned, with the clue shown earlier; here the clue is the inked page.
