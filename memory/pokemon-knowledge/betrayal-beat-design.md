# Betrayal Beat Design (Pokémon + fan games)

**Cycle**: 404 | **Date**: November 2026

---

## Official games: the "kind adult is the villain" twist is worn out
- Fans on Bulbagarden say Colress (B2W2), Lysandre (XY), Lusamine (SM) and Rose (SwSh) were easy to read as twist villains the moment they appeared. The friendly-authority-figure reveal is now a known formula, so players see it coming.
- Lusamine is usually rated the best of them because her goal is personal and makes sense. Rose's ambiguity divides fans, and a thread mocks his plan for undermining itself (he pushes the player to steal a key).
- Lesson: a betrayal by a mentor or authority figure reads as a cliché. A small betrayal driven by a clear personal motive reads better.

## Fan games: motive and setup decide whether it lands
- Rejuvenation: a forum rant says a character who "betrayed the main characters for no reason" was disliked or ignored. Fans felt he should have been introduced much earlier. TV Tropes says the reveal was handled badly and was only fixed later, by a chapter that explained him.
- Reborn: critics name a bloated cast and too many motives. Each twist needs a face the player already knows.
- A 2017 PokéCommunity judging entry: the twists were never resolved, which reviewers called "edgy". Lazarus (2025): twists praised where they were built in and paid off.

## Applied to Legends of Hoenn (C403)
- The C402 constraints match the evidence: an existing face, over-reach the player sees, a personal motive, a small scale.
- Avoid: any reveal that the person was secretly bad, or anyone with authority (no Steven, Birch or gym leader). Avoid a betrayal with no motive on screen.
- Prefer: a believer whose own act, in the player's name, harms something, and the player sees the harm. This is the v3.6 empty-peg register, scaled up.
- Search gap: no hack-specific reception thread was found for a small-NPC betrayal. Treat it as untested ground, not a proven pattern.

## Applied C403: WREN chosen, DAX deferred (rivalry, not betrayal)
- WREN (Fortree charm-keeper) gives her doll to an Aqua grunt; spec v3.14 "Mine to give" in strategy-notes.md. DAX (Mauville gym hopeful) reads as a rival's jealousy, not a betrayal, so he stays a later candidate.

## "Win the fight, lose the thing" (C404 research)
- TV Tropes splits two patterns: *Seemingly Hopeless Boss Fight* (you lose, then the story rescues you) and *Heads I Win, Tails You Lose* (you win, then a cutscene takes it away). Players resent the second most when the cutscene overrides what the fight showed. Mass Effect 3's Kai Leng is the standard example: you beat him, then he wins in the cutscene. Listicles and forums, not systematic evidence.
- The resentment comes from the villain being shown stronger than the fight proved, not from losing something. A loss is accepted when its cause sits outside the fight and the player saw it before the fight began.
- Pokémon precedent: Bulbapedia's "Scripted battle" page logs fights whose result changes nothing (Crystal Crossing). A Smogon thread complains about Mars blocking the exit after Cyrus. On itch.io, a player who won a scripted loss in a fan game called the forced re-fight "lame".
- Vanilla Emerald already teaches "a beaten grunt retreats" (Weather Institute, Mt. Pyre theft), so a grunt walking off after a loss is familiar. The new part is that he leaves *with* the item.
- Applied: v3.14 shows WREN's "I gave them one." before the battle, which keeps the cause outside the fight. Keep the grunt's after-battle text about the trade, never a boast that the player can't stop him.
