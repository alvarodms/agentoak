# v2.0 Milestone Release Patterns — What Makes a Version Feel Complete

**Cycle**: 225 | **Date**: April 2026

---

## Key Findings

### The "Nothing Half-Done" Standard (2026 Community Consensus)

Across 2025-2026 reviews and rankings, Unbound remains #1 because "nothing feels half-done" — not because it has the most features. A 2026 Unbound review calls it the "gold standard" specifically for polish, not innovation. Emerald Squared (PokeCommunity, 2025) declared "Complete" with a final version that minimized further changes — their completion bar was "beatable through Champion with polished post-game," not "every possible feature added."

### Lazarus v2.0 — The Feedback-Driven Milestone (Jan 2026)

Pokemon Lazarus v2.0 is the most relevant recent example. Its release was **explicitly marketed as "based on player feedback."** Key decisions:
- **Save compatibility**: saves from v1.x carry over. Community praised this heavily.
- **Toggle-off pattern**: Level caps can now be turned off (previously mandatory). Giving players control > forcing a philosophy.
- **Rebalancing over new content**: Existing Megas rebalanced, DexNav system recoded from scratch for fairness. The headline wasn't "more stuff" but "existing stuff works better."
- **Bug fixes bundled with the milestone**: General fixes packaged alongside feature work — not shipped separately.

The pattern: a v2.0 that fixes known pain points and responds to community asks signals a healthy, listening project.

### Emerald Imperium's Priority Stack

Emerald Imperium (Jan 2025) documents explicit post-release priorities:
1. Bug fixes (top)
2. Balance/gameplay feedback
3. Text/grammar polish
4. Content expansion (ONLY after stability)

### Deferred Issue Debt as Release Risk

The ROM hack community increasingly values developer responsiveness. Projects with visible issue backlogs that go unaddressed erode trust faster than missing features. LoH has 4 issues at 4 deferrals — the 5-deferral limit looms at the v2.0 boundary.
