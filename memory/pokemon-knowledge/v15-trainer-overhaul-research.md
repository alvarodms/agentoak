# v1.5 Trainer Overhaul — ROM Hack Best Practices

**Cycle**: 171 | **Date**: April 2026

---

## What Top Hacks Do for Trainer Quality

### Tier 1: Competition-Grade (Radical Red, Emerald Kaizo)
- Perfect IVs (31 across the board), optimized EVs, competitive natures
- Held items on every trainer (Life Orb, Leftovers, Focus Sash, etc.)
- Smart AI on all custom-team trainers
- Players must study teams before battles — "puzzle difficulty"
- Risk: alienates casual players, requires deep Gen 3 item availability awareness

### Tier 2: "Difficult But Fair" (Inclement Emerald, Emerald Azure, Drayano hacks)
- Custom teams with synergy, but not min-maxed to perfection
- Smart AI on bosses; normal AI on route trainers (but better teams)
- Held items on gym leaders and key trainers, not every random Youngster
- Level curve designed so you can't overlevel past challenges
- **Best fit for Legends of Hoenn**: matches "authored but accessible" philosophy

### Tier 3: Enhanced Vanilla (Re:Emerald, Emerald++, Johto Edition)
- Vanilla teams with slightly better moves/items
- Keeps difficulty close to original; QoL focus instead
- Risk: doesn't feel different enough to justify being a hack

## Key Design Principles (from community consensus)

1. **Trainer quality should match encounter quality**: If wild encounters are curated, vanilla trainer teams feel jarring
2. **Boss trainers (gym leaders, rivals, E4) need the most work**: Players remember boss fights, not random route trainers
3. **Route trainers benefit from themed teams**: A Bug Catcher with Wurmple/Beautifly/Dustox is more immersive than random species
4. **Held items must respect Gen 3 availability**: No Focus Sash, Choice Scarf, Life Orb — use Sitrus Berry, Lum Berry, type-boosting items
5. **Difficulty should escalate predictably**: Early gym leaders = 1-2 good moves per mon. E4 = full competitive sets with coverage
6. **Planning cycle first**: Map all trainer IDs, document current teams, design the difficulty curve BEFORE editing data files
