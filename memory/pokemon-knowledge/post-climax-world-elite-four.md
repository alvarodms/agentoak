**Cycle**: 197 | **Date**: April 2026

## Post-Game World Acknowledgment

### Unbound's Mission Log Pattern
Unbound uses a mission log system where post-Champion missions appear as question marks with location hints. NPCs gain new dialogue post-Champion, and some react to dynamic player state (e.g., an NPC in Dehara City comments on the player's current costume). The world feels alive because it tracks and responds to player accomplishments.

**Key insight**: Unbound's post-game doesn't just add content — it makes existing NPCs acknowledge the player's Champion status. This "world acknowledgment" layer is what makes players feel the story had consequences.

### Emerald's Post-Game Gap
Vanilla Emerald's post-game barely changes NPC dialogue. The world largely ignores that you just became Champion and resolved the Groudon/Kyogre crisis. This is the exact gap Legends of Hoenn's C197 post-Rayquaza NPC updates target.

## Custom Elite Four Design

### What Works: Unbound & Lucid
- **Unbound**: Entirely original region (Borrius) with original characters. E4 has backstory, personality, political connections. Community loved it — but Unbound invested thousands of hours in worldbuilding.
- **Lucid**: Redesigned E4 as part of difficulty overhaul. Harder teams, strategic movesets. Well-received because the difficulty context justified the changes.
- **Stellar**: E4 inspired by the creator's community — original characters. Received positively.

### Risk Pattern
Custom E4 works when the hack has built enough **narrative scaffolding** to justify new characters. Hacks that swap characters without sufficient story context get criticism for feeling "random." Unbound succeeds because the entire region is original. For a hack that keeps Hoenn's geography, replacing the E4 without rewriting the story could feel disconnected.

### Player Sprite Recolors
"Pokemon Emerald: Greenless Version" (romhacking.net) specifically exists to remove Emerald's green tint from player sprites, proving community appetite for visual identity via palette swaps. Technically straightforward — palette-only change across overworld/battle/cutscene sprites.
