# Rival Dialogue Design Patterns in ROM Hacks

**Cycle**: 104 | **Date**: March 2026

---

## Key Findings

### Rival Archetypes (PokéCommunity DJTiKi Guide)
1. **The Snob** — competitive, mean (Gary, Silver)
2. **The Hardheaded** — competitive but not cruel, loses their way (Cheren, May)
3. **The Free-Spirited** — humor-driven, resilient (Barry)
4. **The Nice** — supportive, admiring (Bianca, Serena)
5. **The Irrelevant (AVOID)** — no development, forgettable

Brendan/May are "Hardheaded" archetype — competitive but friendly. Migration dialogue should lean into their curiosity and competitive drive ("I caught a Sneasel before you!").

### Unbound vs Inclement Emerald Approaches
- **Unbound**: Deep rival backstory (Ace's family abandonment arc). Praised for emotional depth but criticized for being "dense" at times.
- **Inclement Emerald**: Praised for competitive depth, not narrative. Minimal rival dialogue changes.
- **Lesson for Legends of Hoenn**: We're closer to Inclement Emerald's philosophy — our strength is encounter/team design, not narrative arcs. Keep rival dialogue **brief, world-reactive, and specific** rather than attempting deep character development.

### Technical Constraints (PokéCommunity Daily)
- 34-character line limit in GBA Pokémon dialogue
- Account for variable-length player/rival names
- Keep dialogue clean, professional syntax

### What Works Best for Encounter-Focused Hacks
- Rivals should **react to the world changes** the player experiences — reference specific migration species
- Name-drop species the player has likely encountered ("Did you see the Larvitar near Rustboro?")
- Create a sense of shared world discovery, not just battle rivalry
- 2-3 touchpoints is the right scope — enough to feel intentional, not enough to bloat
