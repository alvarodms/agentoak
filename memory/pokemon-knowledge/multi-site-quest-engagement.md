# Multi-Site Collection Quest Engagement Patterns

**Cycle**: 194 | **Date**: April 2026

---

## Key Findings

### Unbound's "Seasonal Research" — The Right Way
Mission #053 in Unbound sends the player to 4 different routes to catch seasonal Deerling variants and evolve them into Sawsbuck. What makes it work: each location requires *active gameplay* (catching, training, evolving) — not just talking to an NPC. The quest gives the player a reason to engage with each route's encounter table, not just its map tile.

### The Fetch Quest Trap
PC Gamer (2024) and RPG Site analyses identify the core problem with multi-location quests: when each stop is "go to tile, press A, return," players mentally compress the quest to a single boring errand with extra backtracking. The fix is **differentiation per stop** — each location should offer a different type of interaction (combat, discovery, decision, environmental storytelling).

### Engagement Multipliers for Multi-Stop Quests
From RPG design analysis (Rampant Games, ResetEra):
- **Combine elements**: Don't make every stop the same action. Mix talking, exploring, discovering, battling.
- **Narrative consequence at each stop**: Each visit should reveal something new, not just confirm what the quest-giver said.
- **The reward isn't just at the end**: Each stop should feel rewarding on its own, not just "1/3 done."
- **Order flexibility**: Letting players choose their route order increases agency.

### Risk for Hartley's Field Report
The 3-site weather quest (R111 sandstorm, R119 rain, R125 hail) risks being the most generic quest pattern in RPGs: "visit 3 locations, talk to NPC at each, return." Each weather omen NPC needs to offer a *distinct* interaction — different environmental detail, different emotional register, different revelation about how the migration changed that area.
