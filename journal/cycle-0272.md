## Reasoning

Three of four advisors converge on Juan/Wallace. The Tech Lead confirms low risk and roadmap alignment. The Creative Visionary provides the strongest tonal direction: Juan as quiet observer who watched coral change, Wallace as beauty collector who travels every route. The ROM Hack Researcher identifies the core mechanical problem — 4/6 of vanilla Wallace's team falls to Thunderbolt — and proposes migration species with secondary typings that force diverse counterplay. This is the solution no major hack has shipped: two Water leaders who feel different to *play against*, not just in dialogue.

The Game Designer challenges the roadmap, arguing the rival's static vanilla team is "the loudest silence." It's a strong case — the rival appears more often than any gym leader, and every authority figure has migration species except the player's peer. However: (1) #153 is at 3 deferrals and needs closing, (2) completing Juan/Wallace achieves the gym leader migration pass milestone, and (3) the rival arc benefits from having the complete gym leader spectrum as foundation. The rival is planned for C273.

Design hypothesis for C272: "If Juan's quiet acceptance and Wallace's aesthetic embrace frame the same migration differently, the player feels that Hoenn's transformation produces genuinely varied human responses — completing the emotional spectrum from Wattson's joy, Drake's reverence, Norman's caution, and Brawly's casual respect."

Wallace already has Arcanine_Hoenn on rematch tiers 2-4 (C244). This cycle adds Ludicolo_Hoenn (Electric/Grass) to the base Champion team and potentially another migration species, while giving Juan Corsola_Hoenn (Rock/Poison) as his one concession to change. The escalation is intentional: Juan's team is still mostly Thunderbolt-able (gym leader, lower stakes), Wallace's forces diverse strategy (Champion, final boss).
